- Probe kinds: method and constructor (`method: new`), points `entry`, `exit`, `exception`; line
  (`line: [...]`, no `at`); call site (`call: {...}`), points `before`, `after`, `exception`.
- `class` is a fully qualified name or a wildcard ('com.shop.*Service'); `subtypes: true` also matches
  subclasses and implementations. `method` without `params` covers every overload; `params: [String, int]`
  selects overloads by parameter types written as in the source.
- `when` and `what` are CEL expressions over `this`, `args` (`args[0]`, ...), `vars.<name>` (parameters
  and local variables by source name), `ret` (at `exit`, `after`), `exception`, `duration` (ms) and, at a
  call site, `call.target` and `call.args`. A `what` item is a bare expression, keyed by its text, or
  `name: expression`. Quote expressions that contain YAML special characters.
- Values are read from fields; program methods never run. JDK strings, numbers, `BigDecimal`,
  `java.time` types, enums, collections, maps and arrays are plain JSON; other objects are maps of their
  fields with `"@type"`.
- `vars` needs classes compiled with `-parameters` or `-g`; local variables at a line need `-g`. An item
  that does not exist at a point is left out of that record; a key with `null` means the value was null.
- `limits` (defaults): `depth` 3 (levels of object fields), `items` 100, `string` 1000, `fields` 50,
  `max_hits` 1000 (hits after which a probe stops firing). A value cut by a limit is marked
  (`"@truncated": true`, `"@size"`, `"@prefix"`); an object met twice in one record is `"@id": N` the
  first time and `{"@ref": N}` later.
- Output: one JSON record per line: `run`, then `hit` and `diag` records, then `end`. A hit has
  `probe`, `point`, `method`, `line`, `call` (shared by the records of one execution, e.g. its entry and
  exit), `parent` (the call of the enclosing probed execution), `thread`, `pid` and `what` (the keys
  of the spec). A `diag` explains placement (placed, no class matched, a name not available); `end`
  has the hits per probe and `code`, the exit code of the scenario.
